1\. How to use this script – step by step

1\. Pick your tools. You need (a) an image generator that accepts reference images, such as ChatGPT image

generation, Google Gemini or Flow, Midjourney, Leonardo, Ideogram or Higgsfield; (b) an image-to-video animator, such as Kling, Runway, Hailuo, Higgsfield or Google Flow; (c) a voice tool, such as ElevenLabs or CapCut text-to-speech; and (d) an editor, such as CapCut.

2\. Set the format. Everything is vertical 9:16. Generate images at the tool's tallest 9:16 size and export the final

video at 1080 × 1920, 30 fps.

3\. Lock the style once. Paste the MASTER STYLE (section 2) at the start of each chat-based image session. Every

scene prompt in this script already carries its own Style line, so it also works in tools that do not remember context. If your tool has a negative-prompt box, paste the NEGATIVE PROMPT there.

4\. Make the end card first. Generate the Storyteller end card (section 2.6) once. You reuse the same image and clip

as Shot 12 in every part.

5\. Make character sheets before any scenes. For each series, paste every Character Sheet prompt, generate 2–4

options and keep the best one. Save it as e.g. ‘C1\_GURU.png’. This is the single most important step for keeping characters identical across parts.

6\. Make location plates. Paste each Location prompt (empty background, no characters). Save the best one. Use it

as a background or style reference for the shots in that place.

7\. Generate the shots in order. For each shot, paste the IMAGE PROMPT and attach the matching character

sheet(s), plus the location plate if your tool allows several references. Generate 2–4 options, pick the one that best matches the narration line, and check it against the checklist in section 2.8. Name files e.g. ‘C1\_P1\_S03.png’.

8\. Animate each shot. Upload the image into your animator with its MOTION PROMPT (the Motion line plus the

standard motion suffix in section 2.4). Generate a 5 s or 10 s clip and trim it to the shot's duration in the timing column.

9\. Record the voiceover. Paste the part's FULL NARRATION block into your voice tool, using the series' narrator

direction. Aim for about 80–85 seconds of speech. If it runs long, speed it up to 1.05–1.1× rather than cutting lines.

10\. Edit. In CapCut, lay the clips in shot order against the voiceover using the time codes. Add bold auto-captions in the lower third, the TEXT overlays listed in each shot (titles, dates, names, signs), the sound effects, and music at about –20 dB under the voice. Finish with the end card and a ‘FOLLOW – Part X next’ button.

11\. Match the reference video's pace. The reference cuts roughly every 3 seconds. For any shot longer than 6 s,

split it into two cuts: the full frame, then a 120–130 % punch-in on a face or detail. You can also regenerate the same image with a different expression.

12\. Export and post. Export at 1080 × 1920, 30 fps. Turn on the platform's AI-generated content label. Use the post

caption given for each part, and post the parts in order (daily works well).

2\. Master style guide

2.1 Master style (set-up prompt)

MASTER STYLE (use this to set up the look, and paste it before your first prompt in any chat-based image tool): A polished 2D cartoon stick-figure animation style for vertical short-form video. CHARACTERS: every person has a large, perfectly round, pure-white head drawn with a thick, smooth black outline (no skin colour, no shading on the head except a faint grey under-shadow). Faces are simple but very expressive: big white eyes with solid black pupils, thick black eyebrows that carry the emotion, and a clear mouth shape (grin, scream, frown, gritted teeth). Bodies, arms and legs are thin, solid-black stick lines of even weight; hands are round black mittens with simple fingers when needed; feet are small black ovals. Hair, hats, beards and clothes sit on top of the stick body as simple flat-colour shapes with black outlines, so each character is recognisable from its silhouette. Use exaggerated cartoon emotion symbols when the moment calls for it: blue sweat drops, shake lines, motion puffs, anger steam, sparkles, question marks, a lightbulb. BACKGROUNDS: detailed, flat-colour, cel-shaded cartoon environments with clean dark outlines, soft gradients, believable furniture and props, and warm motivated light sources (lamps, candles, windows, moonlight, sunsets). Gentle vignette. COMPOSITION: vertical 9:16, characters large enough to read on a phone, main action in the middle two-thirds of the frame, clear space in the lower third for captions. NEVER: photorealism, 3D, anime, skin-coloured heads, text or watermarks.

2.2 Style line used inside every scene prompt

Style: 2D cartoon stick-figure animation still. Every character has a big, perfectly round, pure-white head with a thick, clean black outline and a simple, expressive cartoon face (large white eyes, solid black pupils, bold black eyebrows, clear mouth shape). 2.3 Flashback / memory style Used for memories and imagined scenes (marked FLASHBACK in the shot list), like the grey pencil-sketch segment in the reference video. {accent} is filled in per shot.

Style: the same 2D cartoon stick-figure look, rendered as a greyscale pencil-sketch flashback: soft graphite shading, off- white paper tone, slightly faded edges. Everything is grey except {accent}, which stays in full colour. Round white heads with thick black outlines, thin black stick limbs. Vertical 9:16 frame. No text, no letters, no logo, no watermark.

2.4 Negative prompt and motion suffix

NEGATIVE PROMPT: photorealistic, photo, 3D render, CGI, realistic skin, skin-coloured head, human anatomy, anime, manga, chibi, missing outline, extra limbs, extra fingers, extra heads, deformed face, crossed eyes, blurry, low resolution, text, letters, words, numbers, captions, speech-bubble text, logo, watermark, signature, frame border, split panels

ADD TO EVERY MOTION PROMPT: Smooth, simple 2D cartoon animation. Keep every face, outline, colour and proportion exactly as in the image. No morphing, no new characters, no text.

SHORT STYLE (for tools with strict prompt limits, e.g. Midjourney; add --ar 9:16): 2D cartoon stick-figure style, round pure-white heads with thick black outlines, expressive faces, thin black stick limbs, detailed flat cel-shaded background, warm lighting, 9:16, no text

2.5 Technical specification  Frame: 9:16 vertical, 1080 × 1920 final, 30 fps.  Runtime: 90 seconds per part = 11 story shots (85 s, timed to the narration) + 1 end card (5 s).  Narration: about 150–200 words per part, roughly 2–2.4 words per second, which leaves room for sound-effect

beats.  Captions: large bold white text with a black outline, 2–4 words per line, in the lower third (the prompts keep that

area clear).
