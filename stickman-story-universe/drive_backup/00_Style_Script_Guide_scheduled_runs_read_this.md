# STICKMAN STORY UNIVERSE – STYLE & SCRIPT GUIDE

Every episode: 45–60 seconds, narrated, stickman style, about 130–160 words of voice-over, 7 story scenes + 1 end-card scene.

## Five-beat formula

1.  HOOK (0–3 s): a surprising first line that stops the scroll.
2.  STORY (3–40 s): 5–6 quick, very visual beats; every line must be drawable.
3.  JOKE: one gentle, modern laugh in the middle (deadpan aside, funny reaction).
4.  LESSON (last 8 s): one clear, powerful sentence.
5.  TEASE (last 3 s): 'Next time: . Follow so you don't miss it\!'

## Rules

  - Retell in your own words from the public-domain source; never copy modern books, videos or other creators.
  - Bible: humour only about people's reactions, never about God or Scripture; give the reference.
  - Nasreddin: gentle, respectful tone (Muslim folk tradition).
  - Violence always off-screen (cut away). No gore. Nothing sexual.
  - British spelling.

## Style line (append IN FULL to EVERY image prompt)

Style: 2D cartoon stick-figure animation still. Every character has a big, perfectly round, pure-white head with a thick, clean black outline and a simple, expressive cartoon face (large white eyes, solid black pupils, bold black eyebrows, clear mouth shape). Bodies, arms and legs are thin solid-black stick lines with round black hands and small oval black feet. Clothes, hair and hats are simple flat-colour shapes with black outlines. Detailed flat-colour, cel-shaded cartoon background with soft gradients and warm, motivated lighting. Vertical 9:16 frame. No text, no letters, no logo, no watermark.

## Negative prompt

photorealistic, photo, 3D render, CGI, realistic skin, skin-coloured head, human anatomy, anime, manga, chibi, missing outline, extra limbs, extra fingers, extra heads, deformed face, crossed eyes, blurry, low resolution, text, letters, words, numbers, captions, speech-bubble text, logo, watermark, signature, frame border, split panels

## Motion suffix

Smooth, simple 2D cartoon animation. Keep every face, outline, colour and proportion exactly as in the image. No morphing, no new characters, no text.

## Recurring characters (include the matching description in any prompt where they appear)

  - Socrates: a stickman with a big round white head, a bald top, a huge bushy grey beard, a snub nose, bulging curious eyes, a plain off-white robe over one shoulder, bare feet
  - Xanthippe, Socrates' wife: a stickwoman with dark hair in a tight bun, fierce eyebrows, a terracotta-coloured robe, hands on hips
  - Crito, Socrates' loyal rich friend: a stickman with neat brown curls, a short beard, a fine blue robe, a worried face
  - Nasreddin: a stickman with a huge round white turban, a short grey beard, a mischievous grin, a striped green robe and pointy slippers
  - Nasreddin's donkey: a cartoon grey donkey with big ears and a stubborn face
  - Noah: an old stickman with a long white beard, a brown robe and a carpenter's hammer
  - Abram/Abraham: an old stickman with a long grey beard, a striped desert robe and a shepherd's staff
  - Sarai/Sarah: an old stickwoman with a grey head covering and a kind, wrinkled smile
  - Lot: a middle-aged stickman with a black beard and a red robe
  - Anansi: a cartoon spider stick-figure with a round white head, eight thin black legs, a tiny straw hat and a sly grin
  - The Storyteller (host for Bible, Aesop, Anansi, Grimm): a friendly stick-figure host with a big round pure-white head, a warm wide smile, small round black reading glasses, a short red knitted scarf and a thin black stick body, holding an open storybook
  - New recurring characters (e.g. Moses, David, Jesus, Peter): define a short, fixed description the first time they appear and reuse it word for word in later episodes.

No money, no shoes, and the most annoying questions in history. One day, his friend went to the famous Oracle at Delphi and asked: 'Is anyone wiser than Socrates?' The answer came back: 'No one is wiser than Socrates.' Socrates was shocked. 'Me? I don't know anything\!' It was like googling yourself and finding you're a genius. So he went looking for someone wiser, to prove the Oracle wrong. He questioned the smartest people in Athens. They all thought they knew everything. They didn't. Then he understood. He was wiser only because he knew that he didn't know. Wisdom starts with admitting what you don't know. Next time: Socrates tests the politicians. It does not go well. Follow so you don't miss it\!
