# From Private Company to Public Company

Episode ID: RCF-04-PRIVATE-TO-PUBLIC · Series: ROAD TO THE EXCHANGE · 2/4 · Duration: 203.8 s

## Narration script (as recorded)

**[1] The Road to the Stock Exchange · Part 2 – From private company to public company** (0.4s)

Most Zimbabwean businesses start life as private companies. To list, you first have to become public. Here's how the law handles that change. This is Reian Creative Finance. Understanding markets. Following capital.

**[2] The law – Companies and Other Business Entities Act [Chapter 24:31]** (15.0s)

The starting point is the Companies and Other Business Entities Act, chapter 24 31. Section 85 defines a private company, section 86 deals with becoming public, and sections 104 to 114 cover prospectuses. Securities law and the exchange's own rules then sit on top of this.

_On-screen source note: Source: COBE Act [Ch. 24:31], ss.85, 86, 104–114._

**[3] What has to change – The three private-company limits** (36.9s)

Why can't a private company list? Look at its three limits. Its shares can't move freely, but listed shares must trade freely. It can't have more than 50 members, but the Z S E main board needs at least 300 public shareholders. And it can't invite the public to buy shares, which is exactly what an I P O does.

_On-screen source note: Sources: COBE Act s.85; ZSE SI 134/2019 s.87._

**[4] Section 86 – The conversion, step by step** (56.6s)

Here's the conversion under section 86. The board recommends the change and the shareholders resolve to alter the company's articles, removing the section 85 restrictions. From that date the company is no longer private. Within one month, it must drop the word private from its name, and lodge a statement in lieu of prospectus with the Registrar, unless it registers a full prospectus. Your lawyer will confirm the exact type of resolution required.

_On-screen source note: Source: COBE Act s.86. Exact resolution type: confirm with your legal practitioner._

**[5] Board and officers – A public company is held to a higher standard** (82.4s)

A public company is held to a higher standard. Section 195 of the Act deals with directors and section 198 with the company secretary and their qualifications. Exchange rules then add expectations like independent directors, board committees and disclosure duties. We haven't quoted exact minimum numbers, because we couldn't verify them from the official text, so confirm them with your legal adviser.

_On-screen source note: Source: COBE Act ss.195, 198 (section locations). Specific minimums not quoted here because we could not verify them from the official text._

**[6] Your advisers – Who does what** (108.5s)

Who does the work? Your lawyers draft the resolutions and new articles and handle the Registrar filings. Your sponsoring broker prepares you for the exchange. The company secretary keeps the records and board processes in order. And the auditors prepare the financial statements the prospectus will rely on.

_On-screen source note: Practical roles; sponsoring broker requirements per SI 134/2019 ss.16–19._

**[7] A Zimbabwean reality check – Why many firms stay private** (128.0s)

A reality check. Many Zimbabwean family businesses choose to stay private, and that can be the right choice. Going public means founders give up some control and privacy, every result becomes public, and compliance costs money every year. The reward is access to capital from thousands of investors, instead of a handful.

_On-screen source note: General considerations (interpretation by Reian Creative Finance)._

**[8] Do not confuse them – Public company vs listed company** (148.5s)

And don't confuse the two. Becoming public is a legal status under the Companies Act, and you can be a public company without ever listing. Being listed means an exchange has admitted your shares, and you must keep meeting its rules, under the oversight of the exchange and the Securities and Exchange Commission.

_On-screen source note: Sources: COBE Act ss.85–86; ZSE SI 134/2019; VFEX SI 62/2026._

**[9] Going the other way – Public back to private** (167.7s)

Going back the other way is harder. Under section 85 subsection 3, turning a public company back into a private one needs a special resolution and confirmation by the High Court. Delisting is a separate exchange process, which we cover in part four.

_On-screen source note: Source: COBE Act s.85(3)._
