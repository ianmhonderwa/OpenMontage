# Getting Your Company Listing-Ready

Episode ID: RCF-03-LISTING-READY · Series: ROAD TO THE EXCHANGE · 1/4 · Duration: 205.6 s

## Narration script (as recorded)

**[1] The Road to the Stock Exchange · Part 1 – Getting your company listing-ready** (0.4s)

What does it actually take for a Zimbabwean company to get onto the stock exchange? In this four part series we walk the whole road. Part one: getting listing ready. This is Reian Creative Finance. Understanding markets. Following capital.

**[2] Start here – What is a stock exchange?** (16.0s)

First, what is a stock exchange? It's a regulated marketplace where shares in companies are bought and sold. In Zimbabwe you have three venues: the Zimbabwe Stock Exchange, the Victoria Falls Stock Exchange, which trades in US dollars, and FINSEC, an alternative trading platform licensed by the Securities and Exchange Commission in 2016.

_On-screen source note: FINSEC: licensed by SECZ in Dec 2016 (seczim.co.zw)._

**[3] Three kinds of company – Private, public, listed** (38.3s)

Next, three kinds of company. Under section 85 of the Companies and Other Business Entities Act, a private company must restrict share transfers, have no more than 50 members, not counting employees, and cannot invite the public to buy its shares. A public company has none of those restrictions. And a listed company is a public company whose shares have been admitted to an exchange. Remember: public is not the same as listed.

_On-screen source note: Source: Companies and Other Business Entities Act [Ch. 24:31], s.85._

**[4] The foundations – Before anyone will list you** (63.4s)

Before any exchange will look at you, get the foundations right. Be properly incorporated and registered. Keep clean books, with audited financial statements year after year. Have a real track record and a clear growth story. And tidy up your shareholder register and your governance. Many companies spend two or three years on this stage alone.

_On-screen source note: Practical preparation steps (recommended commercial practice), not a single legal checklist._

**[5] ZSE main board – The admission numbers** (84.2s)

Now the numbers. Under section 87 of the Z S E Listings Requirements, a main board company needs subscribed capital of 10 million US dollars, a satisfactory profit history for the last five years where applicable, at least 30 percent of its shares in public hands, and at least 300 public shareholders.

_On-screen source note: Source: ZSE Listings Requirements, SI 134 of 2019, s.87. Check for later amendments before applying._

**[6] VFEX main board – The US-dollar route** (105.2s)

The V F E X, which trades in US dollars, sets a lower bar under rule 88 of its 2026 rules: subscribed capital of 3 million US dollars, at least 10 million shares in issue, 30 percent public float, and at least 50 public shareholders.

_On-screen source note: Source: VFEX Listing and Trading Rules, SI 62 of 2026, r.88. 'Public' generally means holders of under 5%._

**[7] Other routes in – Not every company fits the main board** (123.6s)

Not every company fits the main board. The Z S E has a development stage route needing 1.5 million US dollars of subscribed capital and no profit history, but with a bold risk warning. Exploration and mining companies can skip the profit test. The V F E X has specialist routes for mining, special purpose acquisition companies, green issuers, property trusts and more.

_On-screen source note: Sources: SI 134/2019 ss.313, 317, 341; SI 62/2026 specialist chapters. Confirm the exact rule before relying on it._

**[8] Your listing team – Who you will need** (147.5s)

You won't do this alone. You'll need a sponsoring broker, who guides the application and deals with the exchange. The Z S E rules require one in the cases set out in sections 16 to 19. Add legal practitioners, auditors, and a corporate finance adviser for the valuation and the offer.

_On-screen source note: Sponsoring broker required in the cases set out in SI 134/2019 ss.16–19._

**[9] The disclosure document – The prospectus** (166.8s)

Then the prospectus: the document that tells investors everything material about the company and the offer. The Companies Act deals with prospectuses in sections 104 to 114, and section 108 makes people liable for misstatements. So every number in it must be right.

_On-screen source note: Source: COBE Act [Ch. 24:31], ss.104–114._

**[10] Part 1 takeaway – Listing starts years before listing day** (186.1s)

So, listing starts years before listing day: clean books, strong governance and the right advisers. In part two, we turn a private company into a public one. This is educational content, not legal or investment advice. Follow Reian Creative Finance for more.
